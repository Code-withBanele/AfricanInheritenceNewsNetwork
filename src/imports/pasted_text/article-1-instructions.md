Banele,

Please treat Article 1 — “A Young Man Listens” as a serious long-form heritage journalism piece. The photographs are not decoration. Each image must support the historical argument being made in the paragraph immediately around it.

The central rule is:

DO NOT PLACE AN IMAGE SIMPLY BECAUSE IT IS AVAILABLE. PLACE IT BECAUSE THE IMAGE BELONGS TO THE PARAGRAPH AND ADVANCES THE STORY.

The article should read visually as well as intellectually.

📍 1. TITLE / OPENING — “A YOUNG MAN LISTENS”

TITLE:
A Young Man Listens

SUBTITLE:
Article 1 of the Manona Series

IMAGE 1 — DR WELE CECIL MANONA

Place the best authenticated portrait of Dr Wele Cecil Manona immediately beneath or close to the title/opening section.

This should be the principal image of Article 1.

Caption:
Dr Wele Cecil Manona (1937–2013).

Where the source is known, add the appropriate photographic credit.

This image establishes immediately that although the article begins with Peddie, the subject is ultimately the life and intellectual formation of Manona.

Rhodes University confirms that Manona was born in Durban Location, Peddie, in 1937, attended the Methodist Mission school and subsequently Healdtown.

Chief Maqoma, one of the most prominent Xhosa leaders of the nineteenth-century frontier wars.

📍 SUBSECTION — “THE MILKWOOD TREE: THE OATH AND THE EDUCATION”

IMAGE 5 — THE MFENGU / FINGO MILKWOOD TREE

This image is essential.

Place the photograph of the historic milkwood tree associated with the Mfengu/Fingo Oath directly beside or immediately after the paragraph:

«“On 14 May 1835, under a white milkwood tree near Fort Peddie…”»

This photograph must NOT be replaced by a generic photograph of a tree.

It must be the actual historic milkwood tree/site associated with the Fingo/Mfengu tradition.

Caption:

The historic milkwood tree associated with the 1835 Mfengu/Fingo Oath near Peddie.

LAND → CATTLE → HOMESTEAD → RURAL LIFE

🚨 CRITICAL IMAGE RULES

1. NO IMAGE MISMATCHING

The Hilltown/Healdtown photograph must remain with Healdtown.

The milkwood tree must remain with the Fingo/Mfengu Oath section.

The Fort Peddie photograph must remain with the Fort Peddie section.

The rural/cattle photograph must remain with the rural economy section.

Do not move photographs merely to improve page balance.

---

2. DO NOT USE GENERIC SUBSTITUTE PHOTOGRAPHS

If we cannot authenticate a photograph, leave the photograph out.

Accuracy is more important than filling every available image slot.

---

3. CAPTIONS ARE PART OF THE STORY

Every historical photograph must have:

WHAT IT IS + WHERE IT IS + WHY IT MATTERS

Captions must not simply say:

❌ “Peddie”
❌ “Healdtown”
❌ “Old building”
❌ “Village”

They must tell the reader what they are looking at.

---

4. IMAGE CREDITS

Every externally sourced photograph must have a proper credit.

Where available, record:

Photograph: [Photographer/Institution/Archive]

If the source is unknown:

DO NOT INVENT A CREDIT.

Rather flag it for us.

---

5. DO NOT CROP HISTORICAL PHOTOGRAPHS AGGRESSIVELY

Historical buildings, memorials and landscapes need enough surrounding context to allow the reader to recognise the place.

---

6. DO NOT OVERLOAD THE ARTICLE

This is a long-form heritage article.

We are not producing a photo album.

The text remains primary.

The photographs should create a visual historical journey:

MANONA → PEDDIE → FRONTIER → RESISTANCE → OATH → RURAL LIFE → MISSION → HEALDTOWN → THE THRESHOLD

That is the visual architecture of Article 1.
